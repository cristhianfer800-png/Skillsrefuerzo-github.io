01 // SECOND CONDITIONAL (GRAMMAR)

Unreal, imaginary, or improbable situations in the present or future.

WHEN DO WE USE IT?

We use the Second Conditional to talk about:

  • Imaginary situations in the present (things that are not true right now).
  • Unlikely dreams or hypothetical events in the future.
  • Giving advice using the phrase "If I were you...".

CORE SENTENCE FORMULAS

Standard Order (Comma required):

IF + Subject + Past Simple, Subject + would / could / might + Base Verb

Inverted Order (No comma):

Subject + would / could / might + Base Verb + if + Subject + Past Simple

CRITICAL RULE: "WERE" INSTEAD OF "WAS"

In formal and standard Second Conditional sentences, we use WERE for ALL subjects (I, he, she, it, we, you, they) instead of "was".

If I were you, I would accept the mission. (NOT: If I was you)

STRUCTURAL EXAMPLES

AFFIRMATIVE (+)

  • If I had a time machine, I would visit the year 3000.
  • She could buy a spaceship if she won the lottery.

NEGATIVE (-)

  • If they didn't live so far away, we would see them every day.
  • I wouldn't do that if I were in your position.

INTERROGATIVE (?)

  • What would you do if you found an alien artifact?
  • Would he travel to Mars if he had the chance?

    07 // REPORTED SPEECH (GRAMMAR)

    How to report what someone else said without exact quotation marks by shifting tenses backward.

    THE GOLDEN RULE: TENSE BACKSHIFTING

    When the reporting verb (said / told me) is in the past, the original verb shifts one tense backward. Quotation marks (" ") are removed.

    TENSE SHIFT DIRECT SPEECH (EXACT WORDS) REPORTED SPEECH (INDIRECT)
    Present Simple
    transforms to
    Past Simple    		 "I play soccer every weekend."
    - Leo    		 Leo said (that) he played soccer every weekend.
    Present Continuous
    transforms to
    Past Continuous    		 "I am studying cybernetics."
    - Maya    		 Maya said (that) she was studying cybernetics.
    Future (Will)
    transforms to
    Would    		 "We will call you tomorrow."
    - They    		 They said (that) they would call me the next day.

    SAID vs. TOLD RULE

    • Use SAID when there is NO personal object:
      He said (that) he was tired.
    • Use TOLD when you specify WHO was spoken to:
      He told me (that) he was tired.

    PRONOUN & TIME / PLACE SHIFTS

    • I / we → he / she / they  |  my / our → his / her / their
    • now → then  |  here → there
    • today → that day  |  tomorrow → the next day
